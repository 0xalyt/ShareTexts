How ShareTexts Protects Your Data

ShareTexts is designed around a simple principle: your data moves between your devices, not through ours. Here's exactly what that means.

In short: Live-room transfers are encrypted in transit. The service issues the room secret, so this is not zero knowledge. Temporary Spaces store content server-side. Saved items remain in your browser. The privacy policy explains these distinctions.

What Happens to Your Content

Live rooms: Content travels directly when possible, with an encrypted relay fallback. Live-room content is not stored server-side. Closing a room does not remove files or items already saved on a device.

Temporary Spaces: Content is stored server-side for the chosen lifetime, from 6 hours to 7 days, or until closed early. Keep your own copy of anything you need.

Saved items: Items you explicitly save remain in this browser until you remove them or clear site data. They do not sync to other devices.

What the Service Sees

The service handles room state and connection metadata, including identifiers, IP addresses, sizes, and times. Temporary Space content is stored server-side; it is not covered by live-room content-storage claims. See the privacy policy for the current model.

How Encryption Works

ShareTexts uses WebRTC, which encrypts all data in transit using DTLS (Datagram Transport Layer Security). This is the same encryption used by video calling and other secure browser applications.

  • All data between devices is encrypted
  • Each transfer uses a unique encryption key
  • The pairing code authenticates both devices before any data is shared

Temporary Does Not Mean Nothing Remains

Live rooms expire unless you enable Stay Connected. Temporary Spaces last for the chosen lifetime or until closed early. Neither closing a room nor closing a space removes copies already saved on a device.

No account, email address, or phone number is required. ShareTexts does not use advertising trackers. Those facts do not mean that the service sees no data or keeps no state.

Limitations

No system is perfect. Here's what to be aware of:

  • Both devices must be online during the transfer — there is no offline mode.
  • Signaling server sees metadata — the service handles identifiers, IP addresses, sizes, and times. It also issues the room secret, so this is not zero knowledge.
  • Relay fallback — if a direct peer-to-peer connection isn't possible, traffic routes through a TURN relay server. The data stays encrypted in transit; the relay only forwards encrypted packets.
  • IP addresses are used for rate limiting — the service uses connection information to prevent abuse. See the privacy policy rather than assuming that metadata is never retained.
  • No protection against screenshot/screen recording — the receiving device can capture content after it arrives.
  • No protection against a compromised browser — if your browser is compromised, the encryption cannot protect you.
